HeatRoute

Switchable thermal connections for spacecraft

We are designing HeatRoute to control the connection between equipment and its cooling or storage path. The goal is strong heat transfer when cooling is needed and greater isolation when that connection would be undesirable.

Intended applications

HeatRoute is intended for equipment whose thermal needs change during a mission. Potential applications include computing payloads, temperature-sensitive subsystems, and controlled connections to thermal storage.

Design scope

Switchable conductance

Change heat transfer between two defined interfaces to provide connected and isolated operating states.

State feedback

Provide feedback so the controller can check whether the commanded thermal connection has been established.

Multiple heat paths

Combine switches into a routing assembly where a payload needs access to more than one cooling or storage path.

Integration requirements

These inputs will determine the design and evaluation scope.

  • Required thermal conductance in the connected and isolated states.
  • Operating temperatures and the expected temperature differences.
  • Switching speed, expected cycle count, and available actuation power.
  • Acceptable behavior following a loss of power, control, or actuation.

Development and evaluation

Planned component development

The switching mechanism remains a design decision. Bench tests will establish conductance, isolation, actuation requirements, and repeatability before a component specification is set.

Failure behavior is part of the thermal design. A mission may need a switch to remain connected, remain isolated, or move to a defined state when control is lost.
