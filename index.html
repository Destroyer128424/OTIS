Our Mission

Make high-performance
computing practical in space.

A capable processor is only useful when the spacecraft can power it and manage its heat. We are building toward thermal systems that coordinate heat storage, heat flow, and workload timing—starting with demanding onboard processors.

The Workload

Useful work, on time

Processing, imaging, and communications create changing demands. Thermal limits can determine when a payload runs and how long it stays active.

The Orbit

Changing conditions

Sunlight, spacecraft orientation, and battery state change through a mission. Cooling and computing decisions need to account for those conditions together.

The System

Every trade counts

Added cooling capability must earn its place in the spacecraft's mass, power, and integration budgets. Our focus is the useful work the whole system can deliver.

Product Roadmap · In Development

Five product lines.
One connected platform.

Our initial focus pairs thermal buffering with orbit-aware control. The roadmap extends to heat routing, integrated cooling assemblies, and the tools to test them. We are seeking design and test partners to shape requirements and validate performance.

01 / Software · Initial Focus

OrbitPilot

Coordinate compute, power, and heat.

Planned onboard control software to schedule flexible workloads around temperatures, battery state, orbital conditions, and task deadlines.

  • Workload and thermal-state coordination
  • Ground replay and onboard integration
  • For compute suppliers and spacecraft teams
02 / Hardware · Initial Focus

HeatBank

Buffer demanding payload bursts.

Planned sealed thermal-storage cartridges to absorb temporary excess heat and release it when cooling capacity is available again.

  • Phase-change storage with sensing
  • Defined thermal and mechanical interfaces
  • For intermittent processing and payload loads
03 / Component · Future Development

HeatRoute

Control where heat goes.

Proposed switches and routing modules to connect or isolate heat paths between equipment, thermal storage, and radiators as mission needs change.

  • Controllable thermal connections
  • Operating-state feedback
  • For payload and thermal-system developers
04 / Subsystem · Future Development

ThermalCore

Bring the thermal system together.

A planned integrated cooling assembly combining heat collection, transport, optional storage, sensors, and control around a customer's compute payload.

  • Cold plate to radiator interface
  • Hardware and controls in one assembly
  • For satellite and hosted-payload integrators
05 / Test Tools · Development Platform

OrbitBench

Measure what a mission can sustain.

Planned test fixtures and software for replaying workload and thermal conditions, measuring operating limits, and comparing designs before flight.

  • Instrumented workload and thermal testing
  • Repeatable evaluations and model correlation
  • For payload teams and test partners

These product lines are in development. Specifications, availability, and mission suitability will be established through testing with partners.

Our Approach

Build around
the mission.

Shared sensing, control models, and interfaces connect our roadmap. Each step starts with a specific mission constraint and a measurable test.

01

Start with the workload

Understand what the payload needs to accomplish, its deadlines, and where thermal or power limits restrict operation.

02

Coordinate the full cycle

Account for heat generation, storage, and eventual rejection alongside available electrical power. Thermal storage shifts heat in time; it still has to discharge.

03

Design for integration

Develop reusable modules and defined interfaces around customer equipment, with a path from evaluation to an integrated subsystem.

04

Prove the system benefit

Compare completed work, operating limits, mass, and energy use against existing approaches under the same mission conditions.
